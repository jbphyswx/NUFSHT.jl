Algorithm

Overview

NUFSHT.jl evaluates a spherical harmonic expansion at arbitrary scattered points by writing it as a Double Fourier Sphere (DFS) bivariate Fourier series and handing that series to a NUFFT. The synthesis operator $A : \mathbb{R}^K \to \mathbb{R}^M$ (SH coefficients → scattered field values) is decomposed as:

\[A = N \cdot F \cdot S\]

StepNameSize in → outCost
Splan_sph2fourier$K \to N_\theta \times N_\phi$$O(l_\text{max}^3)$: FastTransforms' Givens rotations and triangular products

where $K = (l_\text{max}+1)(2l_\text{max}+1)$ is the number of SH coefficients and $N_\theta = l_\text{max}+1$, $N_\phi = 2l_\text{max}+1$.

The bivariate Fourier series

plan_sph2fourier produces the DFS series directly, so the pipeline has no equiangular grid and no doubling.

For order $m$ in the sph_mode column packing, the series S returns is

\[f(\theta,\varphi) = \sum_m \Phi_m(\varphi)\, N_m \sum_i G[i, c(m)]\, \Theta_{i,m}(\theta)\]

with $\Phi_m = \cos(|m|\varphi)$ on the $+m$ column and $\sin(|m|\varphi)$ on the $-m$ column, $N_0 = 1/\sqrt{2\pi}$, $N_{m \neq 0} = 1/\sqrt{\pi}$, and

\[\Theta_{i,m}(\theta) = \begin{cases} \cos((i-1)\theta) & |m| \text{ even} \\ \sin(i\theta) & |m| \text{ odd} \end{cases}\]

The parity comes from the harmonics: the DFS extension of $Y_{\ell m}$ in $\theta$ is $\sin^{|m|}\theta \cdot Q(\cos\theta)$, which is even in $\theta$ for even $m$ and odd for odd $m$, the fact the glide reflection $f(\theta,\varphi) \mapsto f(2\pi-\theta, \varphi+\pi)$ encodes.

_assemble_modes! rewrites those cosines and sines as complex exponentials, giving the mode array the NUFFT evaluates. The $\theta$ axis runs $-(l_\text{max}+1) \ldots l_\text{max}+1$: the coefficient array is a square, invertible representation whose supernumerary slots hold degrees $l_\text{max} < l \leq l_\text{max}+|m|$, and an odd-order column's last row is the sine at frequency $l_\text{max}+1$.

The adjoint

$A^\dagger : \mathbb{R}^M \to \mathbb{R}^K$ reverses the chain:

\[A^\dagger = S^\dagger \cdot F^\dagger \cdot N^\dagger\]

nusht_type1! is $A^\dagger$, the transpose, with no quadrature weights. At scattered points $A^\dagger A \neq I$. Quadrature weights $w_j$ make the weighted adjoint $A^\dagger W$ the analysis,

\[c_k = \sum_j w_j f_j \overline{Y_k(x_j)},\]

exact for a degree-$l_\text{max}$ field when the rule integrates the product of two degree-$l_\text{max}$ harmonics exactly; that is nusht_analyze!(C, f, plan; weights). At scattered points without such weights the analysis is the fit nusht_solve! (also nusht_analyze! without weights).

LSMR inversion (nusht_solve!)

nusht_solve! solves

\[\min_c \; \lVert A c - f \rVert\]

by LSMR (Fong & Saunders 2011) on the Golub–Kahan bidiagonalization of $A$. One $A$ and one $A^\dagger$ per iteration, as for conjugate gradients on the normal equations. LSMR's convergence is governed by $\kappa(A)$, the normal equations' by $\kappa(A)^2$, and it decreases $\lVert A^\dagger r \rVert$ — the quantity the solve reports and stops on — monotonically.

Convergence depends on $\kappa(A)$, a property of the point distribution independent of lmax. For the design matrix over the $l \leq l_\text{max}$ modes at fourfold overdetermination:

Point set$\kappa(A)$Iterations to $10^{-10}$
Golden-angle (Fibonacci) lattice$\approx 1.04$$\approx 6$
i.i.d. area-uniform$3$ – $7$$\approx 40$
Half the points in a small polar cap$40$ – $80$$O(100)$

Note: the fit is over the $(l_\text{max}+1)^2$ modes with $l \leq l_\text{max}$, which is the only $SO(3)$-invariant choice; the supernumerary slots of the coefficient array are excluded from the fit even though synthesis uses them. If the point set does not determine those coefficients ($M$ below $(l_\text{max}+1)^2$), the solve returns the minimum-norm least-squares solution and reports converged = false when it cannot reach rtol.

The real fast path

The field element type passed to make_plan is a statement about the data: Float64/Float32 assert the field values are real, and a real field's mode array is Hermitian, $Z[-k] = \overline{Z[k]}$. Only the $k_\theta \geq 0$ half is therefore stored, $l_\text{max}+2$ of the $2 l_\text{max}+3$ rows, on every backend: halving the $\theta$ axis halves the deconvolution and the upsampled FFT and leaves the interpolation untouched.

How the missing half is supplied splits into two cases. Both NUFFT libraries, through FlowTransformBindings, take the half-spectrum of a transform built for the full $\theta$ axis and supply the rest themselves — with real strengths, so the spreading/interpolation halves too and the forward needs no weights. Direct summation's transform is half-height and complex: its centered rows are labelled $k_\theta - \lceil\cdot\rceil$, which a per-point phase undoes, and the absent conjugate half is paid for by doubling every $k_\theta > 0$ row on the way in. The saving is largest where the mode count dominates the cost.

Forward, the fold is free: writing the half and letting the complex-to-real transform imply the conjugate is exact, with no weights. Its transpose is not. The embedding $E : Z_{1/2} \mapsto Z$, $Z[-k] = \overline{Z[k]}$, is $\mathbb{R}$-linear but not $\mathbb{C}$-linear, so

\[E^\dagger \hat{Z} = \hat{Z}[k] + \overline{\hat{Z}[-k]} = 2\,\hat{Z}[k] \quad (k_\theta > 0),\]

using $\overline{\hat{Z}[-k]} = \hat{Z}[k]$ for real strengths. Hence the diagonal $\{1, 2, 2, \ldots\}$: one at $k_\theta = 0$, which is its own partner, two above it. There is no third case at the top: the $\theta$ axis has odd length $2 l_\text{max}+3$ and so no self-paired Nyquist row. A complex field is not Hermitian and uses the full array with no weights in either direction.

Spin-weighted plans

A spin-$s$ plan synthesizes $f = \sum_{\ell m} {}_s f_{\ell m}\, {}_sY_{\ell m}$ with ${}_sY_{\ell m}(\theta,\varphi) = N_\ell\, d^\ell_{m,-s}(\theta)\, e^{im\varphi}$, written as the bivariate Fourier series $f = \sum_{m',m} G_{m'm}\, e^{-im'\theta} e^{im\varphi}$ that one NUFFT evaluates. On host Float64 nodes $G$ comes from FastTransforms' plan_spinsph2fourier; for any other element type, and on a device, from the Wigner-$d$ recurrence. Both are the same linear map.

FastTransforms stores ${}_s f_{\ell m}$ at row $\ell - \max(|m|,|s|) + 1$ of column $1$ ($m = 0$), $2m+1$ ($m > 0$) or $2|m|$ ($m < 0$), as the coefficient of $\sigma_{ms}\, {}_sY_{\ell m}$, with $\sigma_{ms} = (-1)^{m+s}$ for $m + s > 0$ and $1$ otherwise. Its Givens rotations and Jacobi-to-Chebyshev conversions build the orthonormal functions $(1-x)^{|m+s|/2} (1+x)^{|m-s|/2} \tilde P_k^{(|m+s|,|m-s|)}(x)$, $x = \cos\theta$, each with a positive leading coefficient, and $\sigma_{ms}$ is the sign of $d^\ell_{m,-s}(\theta)$ as $\theta \to 0$. Column $m$ of its output is

\[\frac{e^{im\varphi}}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \sum_{j=0}^{l_\text{max}} B_j \cos(j\theta) \quad (m+s \text{ even}), \qquad \frac{e^{im\varphi}}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \sum_{j=0}^{l_\text{max}} B_j \sin((j+1)\theta) \quad (m+s \text{ odd}),\]

so $G_{0m} = B_0/\sqrt{2\pi}$ and $G_{\pm k, m} = B_k / (2\sqrt{2\pi})$ on a cosine column, and $G_{\pm k, m} = \pm i\, B_{k-1} / (2\sqrt{2\pi})$ on a sine column. The sine row $B_{l_\text{max}}$, of frequency $l_\text{max}+1$, is zero for degrees $\ell \le l_\text{max}$. FastTransforms' rotations and conversion matrices are real, so its transpose is the exact adjoint.
